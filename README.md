# Final Project (35% of your final grade)

## Learning Objectives

* apply parallel computing concepts to improve the performance of a solution
* produce a technical document summarizing research conducted
* present technical material to both technical and non-technical audiences

## Goal
Select a topic that interests you in the parallel computing area based on the content that you have seen so far. There are no restrictions except for the fact that it must be related to the content of the course (i.e. parellel programming or algorithms). You can do this work *individually or in groups of up to 3 people.*

## Sample Ideas

You are certainly not limited to these, but here are some ideas that you may consider.

- take problem which may benefit from being parallelized, generate multiple solutions, conduct an experiment and analyse its results
- take an advanced concept/feature from one of programming approaches presented this term and report on how it is used
- take a supporting tool (like a profiler or debugger) and report on its utility (you should compare the selection with other alternatives)
- take a parallel language/toolkit and compare/constrast it with those covered in the course.
 
# Deliverables


## Presentation (40% - Tuesday June 16)

Your group will present a summary of the resulting research to your peers. It does not have to follow the exact structure of the report but it should present its main ideas. *It should not exceed 15 minutes.* You can assume the audience is at the same level you are (i.e. undergraduate software students). All members should actively participate in the presentation. The presentation must include a demonstration of the tools used.

Given the condensed nature of the course, a complete solution is not required at this point. It is acceptable to state that portions of the work are still in process.

We will try to get a room on-campus to have the presentations be done in-person during class time.  Virtual options will be made available for those who cannot attend. 


## Code (20% - Monday June 22). 

Code (and other artifacts such as experiment results) that support your research must be submitted. It should be well-documented so the reader can see how it relates to your report.  Instructions should also be included so that the reader can reproduce activities done during your research. 



## Final Report (40% - Monday June 22)

It should be approximately 10-15 pages, one and one-half (1.5x spacing). *It must be delivered in .pdf format.*  The structure is up to you (and it will vary depending on the topic). Points that must be present.

* a clear link to the course material
* a survey which motivates your work (from academic and/or industry sources) 
* the team's experiance with the research 
* limitations of the work
* future work (what would you do next if you were granted more time)

The report should be submitted in **Blackboard**.  All other artifacts should be stored within your assigned **GitHub respository**.
